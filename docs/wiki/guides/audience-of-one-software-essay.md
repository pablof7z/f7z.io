# Audience-of-One Software Essay

## Central Argument

The essay's central argument is that the primary cost of off-the-shelf software is loss of operational flexibility — the inability to redirect the tool when requirements change — not primarily vendor power, data ownership, or ideology. The essay's position leaves open whether packaged software actively shapes organizations or merely reflects their willingness to accept standardization; no position should be inferred on this question. <!-- [^e57dc-f1abc] -->

Continuous directability — the ability to change any relevant behavior whenever the user decides — is at least as important as initial customization. <!-- [^e57dc-507d7] -->

## Opinionatedness and Alignment

The essay holds that opinionated tools are better than non-opinionated tools because a narrowly targeted product achieves higher alignment with its intended users than a broadly targeted product. A tool built for one specific user can achieve 100% fit with that user by embodying their exact preferences, workflows, assumptions, and requirements. <!-- [^e57dc-5df4d] -->

The essay's ideal tool is maximally opinionated around one user, maximally aligned with that user's current needs, and fully directable when those needs change. <!-- [^e57dc-4e20e] -->

Opinionatedness and flexibility are not opposites: a tool can be extremely opinionated in its current behavior while remaining completely flexible about what those opinions become next. <!-- [^e57dc-f0d2c] -->

The 50%, 85%, and 100% alignment figures serve as illustrative geometry rather than empirical estimates. The essay's strongest geometric claim is that the aggregate optimum is a synthetic compromise occupied by zero real users — a point where nobody actually lives — rather than the percentage framing itself. <!-- [^e57dc-e55c1] -->

## Directability and Translation Depth

The concept of translation depth gives 'fully directable' concrete teeth: user → tool versus user → support → PM → designer → engineer → roadmap → maybe never. The fewer translation layers between the user's intent and the tool's behavior, the more genuinely directable the tool is. <!-- [^e57dc-532a9] -->

## Why Now

The essay should explain why audience-of-one software is actionable now but was not before, citing the author's own experience as direct evidence that the threshold has moved, while keeping the claim bounded to what the evidence supports. <!-- [^e57dc-3c8b2] -->

## The Real Objection: Stewardship Cost

The essay should engage the real objection — unamortized stewardship burden (maintenance, security, migration, schema evolution) — rather than rebutting the weaker strawman that custom software preserves irrational processes. <!-- [^e57dc-0cb1b] -->

## Structure and Examples

The essay is structured around concrete app-rebuilding examples — a weight tracker (vs MyFitnessPal) and a podcast app (vs Overcast) — rather than being a pure position paper. It should open with one specific app mismatch told concretely enough that a reader feels it, rather than leading with abstract geometry. <!-- [^e57dc-e4e91] -->

The essay's proposed structure is: one app told properly with its specific mismatch, why the mismatch is correct-for-the-average not a bug, the aggregate optimum as a point where nobody lives, opinionated ≠ rigid, why this is possible now (bounded claim), the stewardship cost of owning the tool, and a widening close. <!-- [^e57dc-6a08e] -->
