Receipts.

Structure decides what a system can't do. A receipt is structure aimed the other way — at what it did, and what it's about to.

  • Correct structure should make runtime behavior legible, not just prevent invalid states.
  • Effects must remain tied to the state that created and owns them.
  • Represent effects as commands plus receipts that record what changed and why each command exists.
  • Use uncommitted transactions as dry runs that show consequences before applying them.
  • Receipts explain the past; dry runs preview the future.
  • Legible cause and effect lets people and agents diagnose or operate a system without guessing.
