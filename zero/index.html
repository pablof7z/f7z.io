Zero.

The distance between saying it and having it. That's the whole project.

Every one of these essays is the same argument wearing different clothes. Attention: your hours are the scarce input, so nothing may waste them. Memory: nothing said once may need saying twice. Structure: the wrong thing must be impossible, not discouraged. Citizens: the entities doing the work must carry your intent under their own keys.

Run the chain and it converges on a number: the distance between saying a thing and having it. Zero is the limit — where you speak, and the system understands, remembers, and executes. Everything I build is an attack on some term of that distance. NDK shortens the distance between an idea and a nostr app. NMP, between one codebase and three platforms. TENEX, between your intent and a fleet that executes it. proactive-context, between what you've said across a thousand sessions and what the next session already knows.

I won't pretend the distance is zero today. It isn't close. Half my hours still go to being the message bus between my own agents — routing, re-checking, re-explaining. The gap is the to-do list.

But there's a rule for how the gap closes, and it's the least glamorous belief I hold: the distance isn't what the system claims — it's what you can demonstrate. "Did you actually test it?" is the question I ask agents most, and it isn't pedantry; a claim without a demonstration is a lie with good posture. Real device, real data, my thumb on the actual screen.

Zero is measured, never asserted.

Asymptotes are unreachable and that's fine — direction beats arrival. Every correction captured, every shim deleted, every agent that stops asking twice takes a term off the distance. You don't have to arrive. You just have to be monotonic.

That's the whole project. The work →
